# PropelSaga: information request

**Date:** 21 September 2026
**Why this exists:** most of what separates a good site from an excellent
one is material only you have. Everything below is something we could not
determine from the current website and cannot invent responsibly.

Items marked **BLOCKER** stop us shipping. Everything else improves the
result. If an answer is "we don't have that," say so, and we will design
around the absence rather than leave a gap.

---

## Section 1: figures and claims

**BLOCKER.** We are publishing these under your name. Every one needs your
confirmation, and where a number has a qualifier, we need the qualifier.

| Figure | Currently shown as | Confirm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D365 implementations delivered | 50+ | |
| On time delivery rate | 98% | |
| Active Microsoft certifications | 350+ | |
| Continents with active delivery | 4 | |
| Client retention | 95% | |
| Certified consultants on the bench | 200+ | |
| Years of D365 focus | conflicting: 7+ and 15+ | |

Three specific questions, because the current site answers them
inconsistently and a buyer will notice:

1. **Is "200+ consultants" your own payroll, or does it include an extended
   partner network?** Against 50+ projects and 30+ clients it implies four
   consultants per project ever delivered, on a permanent bench. If the
   number includes a network, we will say "access to 200+" which is both
   true and still strong.
2. **What does "15+ years" count?** Dynamics 365 launched in 2016. If this
   is combined team experience, or includes NAV and AX work before D365, it
   needs that qualifier. The hero currently says 7+ and the stats bar says
   15+, on the same page.
3. **What is the measurement window for 98% on time?** "Across the last
   three years" is far more persuasive than the bare percentage, because it
   tells the reader you are counting something specific.

**Also needed:** is there any figure you would rather we did not publish?
We would rather show four numbers you can defend in a reference call than
twelve that fill a row.

## Section 2: proof and references

**BLOCKER for the case study pages.** Nothing else on a consultancy site
does as much work as one specific, named, verifiable engagement.

1. **Three case studies we can write up properly.** For each one:
   - Client name, or the precise anonymisation you are comfortable with
     ("a $2B European manufacturer" is fine, but we need to know it is true)
   - Sector, geography, number of entities
   - Starting system and target module
   - Duration, and whether it hit the committed date
   - One measurable outcome. A number the client's own finance team would
     recognise
   - **Written permission to publish**, in whatever form your contract
     requires
2. **Two or three client quotes**, with a real name, role and company. An
   anonymous testimonial is worth close to nothing on a B2B site. If a
   client will not be named, we would rather drop the section than run
   "CFO, Manufacturing Company."
3. **Your Microsoft partner designation.** Solution Partner? Which
   designations? Any specialisations? This is a trust signal in your
   category and it is currently not on the site at all.
4. **Client logos you have permission to display**, with source files.
5. **Any awards, ISO certifications, SOC 2, or security attestations.**

## Section 3: brand assets

**BLOCKER** for the first two.

1. **Logo files.** SVG preferred, plus any variants: horizontal, stacked,
   monochrome, reversed for dark backgrounds. The current site uses the word
   PROPELSAGA set in a font, which suggests there may be no logo file at
   all. If that is the case, tell us, because designing a mark is a separate
   piece of work we would want to quote.
2. **Favicon and app icon** at source resolution. We are currently shipping
   a placeholder.
3. **Any existing brand guidelines**, even a one page PDF.
4. **Photography.** This is the biggest opportunity on the list. We
   deliberately used no stock photography, because a stock photo of a
   generic office next to a specific case study claim is a
   misrepresentation, and every buyer recognises it. What would genuinely
   change the site:
   - Your actual delivery centres and the people in them
   - Named senior people, on the About page, photographed consistently
   - Real screens: a dashboard, a report, a Power BI view, anonymised
   If you have none of this, a half day with a photographer at one office
   would be the highest return spend on this project.
5. **Do you have a house tone of voice document?** If not, we will infer
   one and write it down for your approval.

## Section 4: the enquiry form

**BLOCKER.** Until this is answered the form cannot deliver a lead.

1. **Where should an enquiry go?** A CRM (which one), a shared inbox, a
   webhook, or a form service like Formspark or Basin?
2. **Who receives it and how fast do they respond?** We have written "within
   one business day" on the page. If that is not achievable, we should
   change the copy rather than break the promise on the first lead.
3. **What fields do you actually want?** Currently: name, work email,
   company, and a short description. We removed phone number and preferred
   time because each additional field measurably reduces completion, and you
   can ask for both in the reply.
4. **Should there be a calendar booking integration** (Calendly, Microsoft
   Bookings) instead of, or alongside, the form?
5. **What happens after submission?** Any autoresponder, any sequence? We
   have written "no sales sequence follows the call" on the page, which is a
   differentiator in your category, but only if it is true.

## Section 5: the blog migration

**BLOCKER.** You have fourteen published posts on the live site, dated
December 2024 through September 2026, on strong commercial queries: Copilot
use cases, Business Central versus Finance & Operations, AI costing, user
adoption, CRM adoption. This is your best organic asset and it is currently
absent from the rebuild.

1. **Confirm we should migrate all fourteen**, or tell us which to retire.
2. **Do you have WordPress admin access to hand over**, or an export?
3. **Who writes future posts, and at what cadence?**
4. **Author bylines.** Posts attributed to a named consultant with a photo
   and a credential line perform considerably better than posts with no
   author, both with readers and with search.

Every existing URL needs a 301 to its new address. If the WordPress install
is switched off without that mapping, the traffic is gone permanently.

## Section 6: commercial questions

These shape the copy more than anything else on this list.

1. **What is the actual entry point?** Is there a minimum engagement size?
   A paid discovery? Knowing this lets us qualify visitors on the page
   instead of wasting your sales team's time.
2. **Which of the nine services actually generates revenue?** They are
   presented as equals. They are almost certainly not equals. We would
   restructure the page around the two or three that matter.
3. **Who do you actually lose deals to, and why?** Not "Big 4." The specific
   firms, and the specific objection you hear at the final stage. This is the
   single most useful answer on this form for the copy.
4. **Which geography do you most want to grow?** The site currently treats
   four continents as equal.
5. **Enterprise buyer or Microsoft partner: which is the priority?** They
   want different things and the homepage currently addresses both at once,
   which weakens it for each.
6. **Is there anything you are not allowed to say?** NDAs, Microsoft partner
   marketing rules, client confidentiality.
